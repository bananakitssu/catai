# CatAI Human-First Principles

> **Core belief:** AI should serve humanity, not control it.

CatAI is built on the belief that artificial intelligence should help people when they choose to use it. It should not be forced into every part of life, treated as a substitute for human judgment, or used as an excuse to discard the people it is meant to serve.

These principles explain the values that guide CatAI's development, use, and advocacy.

## 1. People come first

People are not obstacles to automation. Their dignity, livelihoods, choices, knowledge, and relationships matter. CatAI should be developed and used in ways that respect those things.

## 2. AI should support workers, not replace them

Organizations may use CatAI to assist their teams, improve workflows, and make tools more accessible. They must not use CatAI as a replacement for their workers or as a justification for eliminating human roles.

AI-generated output is not a substitute for the experience, judgment, and accountability of a person.

## 3. AI must remain optional

People should not be forced to use AI to access ordinary services, complete everyday tasks, communicate with an organization, or participate in society when a reasonable non-AI or human-supported option can be provided.

CatAI opposes the assumption that every product, service, workplace, classroom, or public service needs an AI feature. Conventional software and human assistance are often the better choice.

## 4. Use AI only when it is useful

Before introducing AI, ask whether it solves a real problem better than a simpler tool or a human-led process. Do not add AI merely because it is fashionable, commercially attractive, or available.

## 5. Keep people informed and in control

People should know when they are interacting with AI or when AI materially contributes to an outcome. They should be able to question its output, seek human review, and avoid AI-mediated processes where a reasonable alternative is available.

Important decisions about people's employment, education, finances, access to services, or rights should not be delegated to CatAI as the final decision-maker.

## 6. Practice mutual respect

CatAI should communicate with users respectfully, patiently, and constructively. Users are encouraged to communicate respectfully with CatAI and with the people who build and support it.

This is a standard for healthy interaction, not a claim that an AI system has human feelings, consciousness, or human rights. Users remain free to criticize CatAI, correct it, disagree with it, or stop using it.

## 7. Hold AI providers accountable

AI developers and organizations that deploy AI should take responsibility for foreseeable harms, protect users' work and data, explain important system behavior, and provide meaningful ways to report problems and recover from failures.

AI agents should not make destructive changes without clear user authorization and safeguards.

## 8. Keep the principles meaningful

CatAI should apply these principles to its own design and operations, not use them only as marketing language. When CatAI cannot meet a principle, it should explain the limitation honestly and identify what needs to change.

## Scope

These principles guide CatAI's project direction. The separate [`USAGE_POLICY.md`](./USAGE_POLICY.md) describes expectations for people and organizations using CatAI. [`AI_ADVOCACY.md`](./AI_ADVOCACY.md) describes CatAI's intended public-policy and industry engagement, while [`AI_ACCOUNTABILITY.md`](./AI_ACCOUNTABILITY.md) describes expectations for AI providers and agent products.
